## 12:30 — claude (Fable) did

The badges. Found by painting every cell fill bright green and looking at what
was actually covered — after four attempts spent adjusting the wrong thing.

AppKit paints `NSBackgroundColorAttributeName` using **each run's own font
metrics**. A badge drawn at any size other than the text's therefore gets a
background of a different height, and the seam between them shows as a tab of
colour or a notch of black depending on which way the difference goes. Scaling
the badge cannot win: shrink it and the gap flips from coloured to black. Three
scales and three baseline offsets were rendered before that was clear.

The console now paints cell fills itself, in a text view subclass, using each
line fragment's full height for every run on the row. That is what a terminal
cell does, and it is why the fill is uniform whatever the glyph beside it is
doing. The fill is stored under a private attribute so AppKit does not also
paint its own, shorter, version underneath.

The green render also showed something worth recording: the badge bitmaps carry
an opaque brand-coloured tile baked into the glyph. They are not transparent
marks on the panel; they are little logo tiles that happen to match. That is
why any mismatch in height was so visible.
