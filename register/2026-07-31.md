**The unreadable-reply failure.** The operator hit it live: one question answered, the next rejected with "The private local guide returned an unreadable reply."

The extraction required the model to print `SLOPNET_REPLY_START` alone on its own line. A 3B model does not reliably do that, and when it wrote the marker inline or skipped it, the whole answer was discarded. Worse, this build of llama.cpp echoes the entire prompt back regardless of its quiet flags — and that echo *contains the marker word*. So matching the marker loosely finds the instruction rather than the answer, which is what my first attempt did: it printed the echoed prompt back at the operator.

Anchoring on the last thing known to belong to the echo — whichever comes later, the marker or the question itself — fixes both. Everything after that point is the reply. Tested against the real model on the operator's server: three questions, three clean answers.

**The reply is a panel now.** It could not be one before because a panel needs a closing border and a live process has no end until it exits. The console can hold a run's output instead of drawing it, and the launcher frames it when the run finishes.

This is for a conversation turn and nothing else, which is the part that mattered most to get right. Setup, installs and sign-ins still stream into the window line by line, because watching them happen is the entire point of showing a terminal. There is a test for each half, and the one asserting that an ordinary run still streams is the more important of the two.

Escape sequences are stripped from collected text before it goes into a panel — a stray SGR from the program would otherwise leak into the panel fill and stain every row after it.

A failed turn is printed plainly rather than framed as the guide speaking. It did not say that; something went wrong.
