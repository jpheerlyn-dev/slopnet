## 03:12 — opus 5 did — one look across the whole app

The console always looked like a terminal; nothing else did. Settings and
Tools were stock dark AppKit — system type, grey rounded buttons, a
system-blue tab pill — so the app read as an office panel bolted onto a
hacker screen. That is item 1 of `PARKED-look-and-feel.md`, and it is done.

`SlopNetBrand` grew a panel-chrome vocabulary: a void backdrop with a crimson
bloom, CRT scanlines and HUD corner brackets; section headers shaped like the
console's own `── TITLE ────`; crimson-edged input boxes; and one terminal
button with three roles. Settings, Tools, the sidebar rows, the tab strip and
Send all use that one button now, so there is a single place to change the
look next time.

Two real bugs fixed on the way. The chrome tint was crimson at 0.42 alpha,
and Liquid Glass lightens what it tints, so the sidebar and composer came out
flat bubblegum pink; it is a deep oxblood at 0.16 now and reads as dark red
glass. Worse, a custom `drawRect:` in these panels was never called at all,
and an undrawn view shows its uninitialised backing store — that is what put
a flat red sheet over the whole Settings window, with the controls faintly
visible underneath it. Everything decorative is drawn from layers now.

Also fixed: infinite recursion in a field-editor delegate that asked its own
window for the editor; a button tracking-area loop that cleared AppKit's own
areas; the Send button sitting clipped and adrift above the typing box; and
the first section heading clipped by the top of the Settings sheet. Tools
gained the provider colour badges it already had the font for, and lost the
blue tab pill in favour of two terminal switches.

The terminal itself was not touched. No glass around the console, no glass
container around the split — the constraint from v0.9.60 holds.

Proved: all seven `checks/*.sh`; unreleased app build; Settings, Tools
Installed and Tools Library looked at as real windows through a scratch
harness that shows those two controllers without a server. Not proved: any
of this against a live server, and no pixel comparison to a mock.

Not done, and waiting on the operator: the Dock icon hue/chroma animation.

## 03:48 — opus 5 did — no system title bar, and the setup guide joins in

Two things the operator asked for after looking at the last pass.

The window had a grey system title bar strip across the top, so the
close/minimise/zoom buttons sat in Apple's colour rather than SlopNet's.
`applyTerminalChromeToWindow:` now sets a full-size content view with a
transparent title bar and a hidden title, so the sidebar's glass runs up
behind those buttons and the window reads as one surface. The catch is that
the top ~28 points stay a drag region even when invisible: a control up there
can be seen and not pressed. The sidebar's wordmark and the terminal tab
strip were both moved down clear of it, and `titleBarInsetForWindow:`
measures that height from the window rather than assuming it.

The setup guide was the last surface still in stock AppKit — system type,
grey rounded buttons, grey separators. It now uses the same panel vocabulary
as Settings and Tools: monospaced body text, crimson headings, crimson-edged
field boxes, the one terminal button with its three roles, phosphor and
crimson status, and the coding-app chooser carries each provider's colour
badge. Its headings are sentence case, not upper: they are questions in plain
English for somebody who finds terminals frightening, and shouting them would
undo the point of the wizard.

One more AppKit trap, logged in PARKED-look-and-feel.md with the others:
assigning a fresh NSClipView to a scroll view brings an opaque grey
background back with it whatever drawsBackground was before. The wizard does
that for its top-anchored clip view, and it hid the backdrop until
drawsBackground was turned off after the swap rather than before.

## 04:26 — opus 5 did — flush edges

The operator looked at the corners and said the edges were sloppy. They were.
Three panels floated inside an eight-point margin of window field, each with
a different corner radius, so every corner showed a dead L of background
between the panel's curve and the window's own. Worse, the console was inset
eight points and the composer four, so the bar under the terminal stuck out
past it on both sides.

The split is flush to the window on all four sides now, the sidebar and
composer are square, and the window's own rounded corners do the rounding.
Console and composer share one edge on each side. The result is two columns
that meet at the divider and run to the glass, rather than cards on a tray.

The padding inside those panels was a bug rather than a choice. Liquid Glass
pins whatever is handed to `contentView` to its own bounds, so the twelve
points of inset `glassPanelWrapping:` thought it was applying were doing
nothing, and the sidebar's rows sat hard against the window edge. The inset
lives in a plain wrapper view inside the glass now, which both paths share.

The password field in the composer was still stock AppKit — the last one in
this window. It uses the panel field box, which also clips, so the system
password badge stops drawing itself outside the field and onto the sidebar.
Show and hide moved from the field to its box, since hiding the field alone
would leave its border behind.

Proved: all seven checks/*.sh; unreleased app build; the main window and its
four corners looked at at 4x, and Settings and Tools re-checked for any
knock-on from the glass change. Not proved: the prompt bar's password state,
which needs a server to ask for one.
